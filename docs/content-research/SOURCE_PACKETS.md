# Source Packets

These notes support expanded guide drafts. They are working notes for owner review, not public comments, user submissions, or a replacement for professional care.

## Shared Source Base

- SAMHSA, trauma-informed approaches: safety, trust, peer support, collaboration, empowerment, and attention to culture/history.
- National Center for PTSD, spirituality and trauma: spiritual or religious beliefs can help, harm, or complicate meaning-making after trauma.
- NCTSN/NC-PTSD, Psychological First Aid: stabilization, grounding, practical support, connection, and avoiding pressure after distress.
- CDC, social connection: isolation and loneliness can affect health and well-being; rebuilding connection matters.
- APA, belief systems and religion: religion/spirituality are psychologically significant and should be approached without simplistic assumptions.
- UNICEF/APA/AACAP child communication resources: children need calm, age-aware, honest reassurance and room for questions.

## Guide Packets

### I Don't Believe Anymore. What Do I Do Now?

- User need: orientation immediately after belief changes.
- Useful sources: SAMHSA trauma-informed approach; NCTSN Psychological First Aid; APA belief systems and religion; Recovering from Religion; Secular Therapy Project.
- Practical takeaways: slow the pace, protect privacy, reduce flooding, choose one safe support option, avoid turning early shock into a permanent life plan.
- Cautions: do not frame doubt as pathology or tell readers to disclose.
- Avoid claiming: everyone leaving religion is traumatized, therapy is required, or one worldview is the healthy endpoint.

### The First 90 Days of Deconstruction

- User need: pacing and stability during early intensity.
- Useful sources: SAMHSA trauma-informed approach; National Center for PTSD meaning-making; NCTSN Psychological First Aid; CDC social connection; Religious Trauma Institute.
- Practical takeaways: stabilize routines, limit overwhelming content, keep decisions reversible where possible, find non-coercive support.
- Cautions: some readers face housing, family, immigration, school, employment, or partner risks.
- Avoid claiming: a fixed timeline applies to everyone.

### Fear of Hell After Leaving Religion

- User need: help with persistent fear after belief changes.
- Useful sources: National Center for PTSD on spirituality and trauma; NCTSN grounding/stabilization; therapist.com overview of religious trauma; Religious Trauma Institute; SAMHSA trauma-informed principles.
- Practical takeaways: name fear as a learned alarm, ground in present safety, avoid compulsive reassurance loops, seek qualified support if fear disrupts daily life.
- Cautions: do not diagnose OCD, PTSD, or religious trauma from a guide.
- Avoid claiming: fear proves belief, fear disproves belief, or exposure work should be self-directed.

### How to Tell Your Spouse You're Deconstructing

- User need: prepare for a high-stakes partner conversation.
- Useful sources: CDC intimate partner violence risk/safety materials; Gottman Institute interfaith-couple communication; SAMHSA trauma-informed principles; APA religion/spirituality resources.
- Practical takeaways: clarify the first-conversation goal, choose timing, reduce debate framing, make room for grief and adjustment, prioritize safety where control or violence is possible.
- Cautions: mixed-belief relationships are varied; some can adapt and some are unsafe.
- Avoid claiming: disclosure is always owed immediately or separation is inevitable.

### How to Tell Religious Parents You No Longer Believe

- User need: decide whether, when, and how to disclose to parents.
- Useful sources: family conflict/boundary guidance from health systems; research on adult child-parent estrangement; SAMHSA trauma-informed principles; CDC safety resources; Recovering from Religion.
- Practical takeaways: assess dependency and safety first, set a modest goal, avoid debate marathons, prepare exit/support plans.
- Cautions: dependent minors and financially dependent adults may need privacy first.
- Avoid claiming: honesty always requires immediate disclosure.

### How to Find a Secular or Religious-Trauma-Informed Therapist

- User need: screen for support that respects autonomy and belief boundaries.
- Useful sources: SAMHSA treatment locators; Secular Therapy Project; Reclamation Collective clinician directory; Religious Trauma Institute; ABCT therapy-choice guidance.
- Practical takeaways: verify license, fit, cost, modality, and crisis policy; ask how the therapist handles religion/spirituality; leave if pressured toward or away from belief.
- Cautions: directories are starting points, not endorsements.
- Avoid claiming: a specific modality or directory is right for everyone.

### What to Do When Church Was Your Whole Community

- User need: rebuild connection after losing a central social world.
- Useful sources: CDC social connection; Harvard Human Flourishing Program; SAMHSA peer support; National Center for PTSD meaning-making; Recovering from Religion.
- Practical takeaways: grief is real, replace functions gradually, diversify connection, start with low-pressure recurring spaces.
- Cautions: loneliness can push people into unsafe groups or rushed disclosures.
- Avoid claiming: secular community must replace church exactly.

### How to Talk to Your Kids After Leaving Religion

- User need: talk with children without alarming them or using them as confidants.
- Useful sources: APA on talking to children about difficult news; UNICEF on age-appropriate reassurance; AACAP child communication resources; Gottman/interfaith family communication; SAMHSA trauma-informed principles.
- Practical takeaways: be calm, age-aware, honest, brief, and repeatable; protect children from adult conflict; allow relationships with safe loved ones where possible.
- Cautions: custody, school, and family conflict can make this legally or emotionally complex.
- Avoid claiming: children must adopt the parent's new belief position.

### How to Rebuild Morality Without Religion

- User need: rebuild ethical confidence after losing divine-command certainty.
- Useful sources: Greater Good Science Center; Harvard Human Flourishing Program; APA moral psychology resources; SAMHSA trauma-informed empowerment; secular ethics education resources.
- Practical takeaways: values can be practiced through care, harm reduction, repair, honesty, responsibility, and community accountability.
- Cautions: avoid replacing one rigid certainty system with another.
- Avoid claiming: religious morality is fake or secular morality is automatically superior.

### How to Handle Holidays, Grief, and Family Rituals After Leaving

- User need: navigate emotionally loaded seasons and rituals.
- Useful sources: National Center for PTSD on spirituality and meaning-making; grief resources from health systems; Harvard Human Flourishing Program on meaning and relationships; SAMHSA trauma-informed principles; CDC social connection.
- Practical takeaways: name the losses, keep what is meaningful if it is safe, create smaller rituals, plan exits and recovery time.
- Cautions: holidays can raise family pressure, grief, financial stress, and safety concerns.
- Avoid claiming: readers must abandon or preserve old rituals.
